Capabilities

Cast the shape. CNC the critical surfaces.

Three casting processes and a CNC machining cell, so each part gets the route that fits its alloy, detail and volume.

Process 01

Die casting

Speed at high volume

Molten metal is forced into a hardened steel die under high pressure. Best for high volumes of thin-wall aluminum parts.

  1. InjectAluminum, zinc or magnesium is shot into the die
  2. SolidifyMetal freezes against the cooled die in seconds
  3. EjectThe die opens and the cycle repeats
  4. FinishTrim, then machine only critical surfaces
Cycle time
Seconds to about a minute
Die life
Often 100,000+ shots in aluminum
Alloys
Aluminum, zinc, magnesium
Typical parts
Crankcases, housings, covers, brackets

Process 02

Investment casting

Fine detail

A wax pattern is coated in ceramic, melted out and replaced with metal. Best for fine detail and hard-to-machine alloys.

  1. PatternA wax copy is molded or 3D printed
  2. ShellRepeated ceramic dips build a hard shell
  3. PourWax melts out and metal fills the cavity
  4. Break outThe shell breaks away from a smooth part
Detail
Thin features, smooth surfaces, little draft
Volumes
Tens to thousands of parts
Alloys
Steel, stainless, aluminum, nickel and more
Typical parts
Impellers, manifolds, complex brackets

Process 03

Permanent molding

Dense and strong

Metal is poured by gravity or low pressure into a reusable steel or iron mold. Best for dense, strong parts at mid volumes.

  1. PrepareThe mold is preheated and coated
  2. PourMetal fills by gravity or low pressure
  3. SolidifyA calm fill gives dense, low-porosity metal
  4. ReuseThe mold opens and goes again
Strength
Denser than die castings, heat treatable
Tooling
Costs less than a high-pressure die
Alloys
Aluminum, magnesium, copper alloys
Typical parts
Cylinder heads, pistons, structural housings

Process 04

CNC machining

The tightest tolerances

5-axis machining centers, live-tooling lathes and mill-turn centers for finished castings, billet prototypes and hardened steel parts.

  1. FixturePart-specific fixtures hold castings or billet
  2. MachineMating faces, bores, mount points and threads
  3. FinishWash, deburr, blast, hone and superfinish
  4. InspectCMM, bore, roughness and hardness checks
Machines
5-axis mills, lathes, mill-turn
Best for
Prototypes, low volume, critical features
Materials
Aluminum billet, alloy and hardened steel
Typical parts
Blocks, pistons, shafts, hubs, plates

Cast or machine?

Casting gets cheaper as volume grows

Machining from a solid block costs about the same per part at any volume. A casting starts near final shape, so cost per part falls as volume grows.

Tooling is a one-time cost. Break-even usually falls between a few hundred and 10,000 parts, depending on the part. We quote both routes so you can see where yours lands.

Example assumes a $50,000 die, $15 per cast and finished part, and $60 per fully machined part. Actual costs depend on the part.

Drone engines

A domestic supply chain for UAV propulsion

We cast, machine, assemble and test UAV engines in Mineral Wells, and we supply engine castings and machined components to drone builders.

Why it matters

The rule has changed

Since July 2026 the Pentagon's stated goal is a wholly domestic small-drone supply chain. Final assembly in the US is no longer enough. Buyers must trace parts back to their origin, and engines, housings and structural metal parts all need US sources.

We start engine programs by machining from billet so parts ship early, then move the right part families to castings as volumes grow and designs freeze. Finned cylinder heads suit permanent mold or investment casting; sumps fit permanent mold in A356-T6.

Complete engines, built and tested hereCasting, CNC machining, assembly and dyno testing in one Texas facility, with traceable records from ingot to finished engine.
